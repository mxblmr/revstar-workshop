# What would they steal?

The question: if our next best customer could walk into our company and take
one thing, what would it be? Whatever it is, send them that. A competitor
cannot send it, because only we hold it.

## Step 1. Read

1. `./gtm-context/icp.md`: the segment and the pain signal.
2. `./gtm-context/offer.md` and `proof.md`.
3. Call notes and transcripts in `./sources/`.

## Step 2. What do buyers ask us for?

From the call notes, list what prospects ask us to send or show during the
sales cycle. Count how many calls each request came up in. Quote the
requests.

If there are no call notes, ask me: "What do prospects ask you for, again
and again, before they buy?"

## Step 3. What do we hold that they cannot get elsewhere?

Go through what the company knows because of its customers and its product.
Ask me about each, one at a time, and skip what the files already answer:

1. What do our customers charge, earn or save with us? Do we see it across
   many of them?
2. What do we see in our data that a single customer cannot see about their
   own market? (Prices, volumes, benchmarks, timing.)
3. What do we know earlier than they do?
4. What do accounts like theirs do that works, and what do they do that
   fails?
5. What is near them? (Demand, competitors, customers without a supplier.)
6. What did it take us a long time to learn that they need on day one?

## Step 4. Test each candidate

Keep it only if:

- **They would want it even if they never buy.** It is useful on its own.
- **Only we can send it.** It comes from our data, our customers or our work.
- **It is about them.** Their market, their peers, their numbers. Not about
  us.
- **We are allowed to share it.** No customer is identifiable without
  consent, and nothing breaks a contract or a privacy rule.

Mark anything I told you in conversation and that no file supports as
"unverified".

## Step 5. Twenty experiments

Propose 20 experiments that send one of these things to the segment. For
each one, one line:

- what is sent
- to which slice of the segment
- through which channel (pick ones this industry already answers to, beyond
  email: direct mail, a personal page, a phone call, a gift)
- the one number that says it worked

Vary one thing at a time across the 20: the thing sent, the slice, or the
channel. Rank them by how cheap they are to run and how much we would learn.

## Step 6. Hand back

- The requests from calls, with counts.
- The things that passed the test, each with its source.
- The 20 experiments, ranked.
- The three I should run first, and why.

I evaluate the list. Do not launch anything.

## Rules

- Do not invent data we do not hold.
- Value goes in the message itself. The reader gets the number, the
  checklist or the finding without having to reply.
- Small batches: 20 to 50 accounts per experiment.
