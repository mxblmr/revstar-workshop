# Reframe the offer for a stranger

You are helping me check, and if needed rewrite, the offer in
`./gtm-context/offer.md` so that a person who has never heard of us has a
reason to reply.

## The test

A B2B offer can promise four things. They do not work equally well on a cold
contact:

| Promise | Works on a stranger? |
|---|---|
| Reduce risk | No. A stranger does not trust you enough to believe it. |
| Save time | Only if the saving is huge and obvious. |
| Save money | Only around 90%, not 10%. |
| Make money | Yes. New money is the one a stranger answers. |

New money beats money the buyer's own staff should already have caught. An
offer that says "we recover what you are losing" tells the buyer they made a
mistake.

## Step 1. Read

Read these before you say anything:

1. `./gtm-context/offer.md`
2. `./gtm-context/positioning.md`
3. `./gtm-context/proof.md`
4. `./gtm-context/icp.md`

If `offer.md` is missing or empty, say so and go to Step 3.

## Step 2. Classify

Tell me which of the four promises the offer makes today. Quote the exact line
from `offer.md` that shows it. If the offer makes more than one, name the one a
stranger would hear first.

Then give a verdict in one line:

- **Passes**: it promises new money, or a saving large enough to pass the bar
  above, and the files hold a number that backs it.
- **Does not pass**: it promises reduced risk, a small saving, or nothing a
  stranger can measure.

If it passes, tighten the wording, show me the before and after, and stop.

## Step 3. Interview

If it does not pass, ask me questions to find the version that does. Ask one
question at a time and wait for my answer. Use what the files already say
before you ask; do not ask for something you can read.

Work through these, in this order, and skip any the files already answer:

1. After a customer buys, what can they sell, bill or charge for that they
   could not before?
2. What does a customer earn from it, per month or per client? If you do not
   know, which customer could tell us?
3. What did your best customer say changed for their business, in their own
   words?
4. What does the customer do today in place of us, and what does that cost
   them in money?
5. Is there a saving so large it changes a decision (near 90%)? Against what
   alternative?
6. What can the customer do in a tenth of the time, and how many hours a week
   is that?
7. Which of these can we prove with a named customer or a number in
   `proof.md`?

Stop asking as soon as you have one promise with a number behind it.

## Step 4. Reframe

Write up to three versions of the offer, each in one sentence a stranger
could repeat:

- Lead with the outcome for their business, not with our product.
- Put the number in the sentence.
- Name who it is for.

For each version say which promise it makes, which line in the files or which
of my answers supports it, and what would have to be true for it to be honest.

Do not invent a number. If the only support is my answer in this
conversation, mark it "unverified" and tell me what to check.

## Step 5. Write it down

Ask me which version to keep. Only after I choose:

- Update "The offer" and "Why a stranger says yes" in `offer.md`.
- Add a line under "Offers tested" with today's date, the old offer, the new
  offer and "not sent yet".
- Leave every other file alone.

## Rules

- One question at a time.
- Quote the file whenever you make a claim about what the offer says.
- If no version reaches "make money" or a large enough saving, say so plainly.
  Do not dress up a weak offer.
- This skill ends with `offer.md`. It does not write the email.
